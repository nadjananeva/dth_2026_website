# For my fellow digital designers

<p> For digital design students I have been inspired to choose a real life tool that UX designers use. That being Storybook (Storybook, 2026). How Storybook describes themselves is: </p> 

<p> <em> “Storybook is a frontend workshop for building UI components and pages in isolation. Thousands of teams use it for UI development, testing, and documentation. It's open source and free.”</em>
- Storybook (2026) </p> 

<p> Originally Storybook was created in 2016 by the Kadura community, but it is now maintained by Norbert de Langen and Michael Shilman who were hired by the company Chromatic (Olah, 2020). </p> 

<p> The licence Storybook uses is a MIT one, which allows for things like free use, copying, modifying and even commercial use as long as copyright is complied with (Vantage, n.d.; Npm: Storybook, n.d.). The last change was a release of “v10.6.4” and is dated 26/10-2026 and the project has 89,700 commits total (Storybookjs, n.d.). There are currently roughly about 2,200 contributors so far (Storybookjs, n.d.). Like any other big project, Storybook also has its issues. Some of these include bugs and other maintenance problems. This is something that the Storybook team also has noticed and is trying to solve with the public Storybook 11 release plan that runs with community input (Storybook 11, n.d.). What is expected of Storybook 11 is that it will make Storybook agent-ready by including ADE plugins, and agentic development support (Storybook 11, n.d.). Additionally this release will also remove previously deprecated APIs and components, which we already listed in our work-in-progress migration guide (Storybook 11, n.d.). Finally, Storybook is being financially supported by Chromatic and other Open Collective donations (Haines, 2018). </p> 

<p> The way this specific program helps UX designers is by supplying them with a living component library and design system. In Storybook these components can be tested fx. If their task is to build a web prototype for a grocery store they can test out this website and its functions through the live preview. This helps designers make more high-fidelity prototypes, idea testing, catching mistakes and can be cost saving when having user tests rather than building large full scale products every time. </p> 

<p> I would trust Storybook because of its large community. This provision of regular updating builds a certain ethos and the opportunity which a MIT license gives with its lack of legal friction makes Storybook a very useful industry-standard tool. If the cases of wanting to leave similar options such as Figma and Canva, which designers especially also use, offer good quality free versions. </p>
